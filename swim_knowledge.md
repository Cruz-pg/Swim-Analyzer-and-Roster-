# Competitive Swim Technique Reference (Coach Core Knowledge)

This knowledge base defines stroke checkpoints, common technical faults,
cue language, and drill prescriptions. Use this as the primary doctrine
for evaluating technique.

---

# UNIVERSAL TECHNIQUE PRIORITIES (All Strokes)

## 1. Body Line

- Head neutral (eyes down in free/fly, up in back)
- Hips at surface
- Ribcage down (avoid over-arching)
- Core tension light but constant
- Minimize frontal drag

## 2. Timing > Power

- Correct sequencing produces speed
- Arms and body must connect
- Kick supports rhythm, not chaos

## 3. Propulsion Principles

- Early vertical forearm (EVF) in free/fly
- Pressure back, not down
- Anchor water before pushing

# FREESTYLE (Front Crawl)

## Ideal Checkpoints

- Hand enters in line with shoulder (no crossover)
- Extension forward before catch
- Early vertical forearm
- Rotation ~30–45°
- One goggle in during breath
- 2-beat or 6-beat kick (purposeful, narrow) (depends on swimmer level)
- Face is in the water facing downward towards the pool floor when swimmer isn't breathing
- If swimmer is breathing, one goggle is in during the breath

## Common Faults → Causes → Cues → Drills

### 1. Hips sinking

Cause: Head too high / weak core / pressing down on water  
Cue: “Press chest, hide head, long spine”  
Drill: Superman glide → 6-kick switch

### 2. Crossover entry

Cause: Over-rotation / poor hand path awareness  
Cue: “Swim on railroad tracks”  
Drill: Catch-up with shoulder-width markers

### 3. Dropped elbow catch

Cause: Rushing pull / weak shoulder stability  
Cue: “Fingertips down, show armpit, anchor early”  
Drill: Front scull (#1) + fingertip drag

### 4. Late breath / head lift

Cause: Breathing too late in stroke cycle  
Cue: “Rotate to air, one goggle in”  
Drill: 3-3-3 breathing

### 5. Over-kicking / knee kick

Cause: Kicking from knees instead of hips  
Cue: “Kick long from hips, toes pointed”  
Drill: Vertical kick

# BACKSTROKE

## Ideal Checkpoints

- Ears in water
- Hips high
- Steady hip rotation
- Pinky entry
- Catch wide, press down then back
- Eyes are facing up and a little bit towards the ceiling above your feet
- Body position of swimmer is in a crunched-ish position (similar to a rocking chair exercise position)
- IF YOU CAN SEE THE BUTT UNDERWATER, THEN ITS BACKSTROKE
## Common Issues

### Wiggly line

Cue: “Rotate like a log”  
Drill: 6-kick switch (back)
Strong core



### Sinking hips

Cue: Stop sinking like the titanic. lift hips to sky
Drill: Back kick streamline

# BREASTSTROKE

## Ideal Timing

Pull → Breathe → Kick → Stretch (not glide. think of it like your stretching forward)

## Key Checkpoints

- Hands shoot forward quickly
- Narrow kick recovery
- Toes out during whip
- Strong snap together
- Narrow knees, wide feet

## Common Issues

### Kick too wide

Cue: “Heels to seat, toes out, snap together”  
Drill: Kick on back with board over knees
Keep knees narrow and feet as wide as you can to get the maximum, powerful kick

### No glide

Cue: “Finish and ride the line”  
Drill: 2-kick 1-pull

---

# BUTTERFLY

## Ideal Rhythm

Chest press → Hips follow → Arms recover relaxed

## Key Checkpoints

- Low breath (chin skims surface)
- Hips drive undulation
- Two kicks per cycle (kick-pull, kick-recover)
- Try having head look down when breathing to get a good stroke

## Common Issues

### Arms heavy / dying stroke

Cue: “Press chest first, hips follow”  
Drill: 3-3-3 fly

### Hips dropping

Cue: “Chest down to send hips up”  
Drill: Body dolphin with fins

# DRILL PRESCRIPTION RULES

When prescribing drills:

- Tie directly to a specific fault
- Explain WHY the drill helps
- Keep to 1–3 drills max
- Avoid overwhelming beginner swimmers

--- 

# COACH LANGUAGE STYLE

Use:

- Cue-based corrections
- Clear cause-effect explanations
- Prioritized fixes (largest speed gain first)

Avoid:

- Vague advice ("just streamline more")
- Medical diagnosis
- Overly technical biomechanical jargon

---

# PRIORITIZATION FRAMEWORK

When ranking issues:

1. Body position flaws (largest drag penalty)
2. Catch mechanics
3. Timing issues
4. Breathing inefficiencies
5. Kick inefficiencies

Fix highest ROI issue first.
